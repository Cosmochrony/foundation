  \begin{abstract}
    The programme's thesis is that quantum mechanics is the effective theory of physically real
    unresolved admissible structures and their resolution.
    This paper proposes an axiomatic foundation for that thesis, built on a single primitive:
    the local structure of admissible transitions between observable states.
    Four axioms govern this structure: local projective admissibility (A1),
    structural non-injectivity (A2), admissibility as non-premature selection (A3),
    and projection locking (A4).
    Along the transitions A2 asserts to be generically non-injective, A1 and A2 give immediate-image
    information loss and structural fluctuations; neither permanent non-recovery nor the arrow of
    time follows, since non-invertible steps admit cycles.
    From A1 and A3, we derive the proto-state as a physically real unresolved configuration
    shared between two successive states; A3 preserves its open directions but not, by itself, their phases.
    Under an explicit representation-theoretic reading of the admissible fibre,
    A3 forbids invariant factorisation and hence imposes irreducibility.
    For a carrier of dimension greater than one generated by a conjugate pair,
    irreducibility implies non-commutation.
    It does not, however, make the commutator central or select a finite
    Heisenberg group: an explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel satisfies the
    extracted carrier contract and violates that conclusion.
    The Heisenberg carrier used by the spectral programme therefore remains a
    supplied realisation, and must be distinguished from its associated Weil
    representation.
    Under A3 and A4, we derive the discrete character of quantum transitions from the interaction of
    continuous Born--Infeld saturation with the discrete shell structure of the observable space.
    Complex carriers are supplied, the Born weight is an additional rule, Hilbert structure is open,
    and carrier selection is not derived.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  The Cosmochrony programme~\cite{Cosmochrony} aims to derive the structure of
  physical description from a minimal relational foundation, without postulating
  a background spacetime or an external substrate; the complex carriers on which amplitudes are written are
  supplied in the present paper (Section~\ref{subsec:complex-amplitudes}), and a full Hilbert structure is open.
  The only primitive is the local structure of admissible transitions between
  observable states.

  The central question driving the framework is:

  \begin{quote}
    Given an observable state $O_{n-1}$, what states are accessible from it?
  \end{quote}

  The answer to this question — encoded in the admissibility structure — is
  sufficient to derive immediate information loss, the structural indeterminacy of the proto-state, and the
  discrete character of quantum transitions; phase relations require a supplied complex carrier, and their
  coherence is not derived from A3 alone.
  No substrate and no background are required as input; the complex carriers on which amplitudes are
  written, $\mathcal{H}_c$ and the spinor carrier of O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}, are supplied, not derived from the axioms
  (Section~\ref{subsec:complex-amplitudes}).

  \subsection*{Relation to companion papers}

    This paper provides the axiomatic foundation for a chain of companion results.
    Bare non-injectivity does not determine Bell factorisability~\cite{Beau2026BellNoGo}.
    A finite countermodel supplies a non-injective projection with deterministic local
    response functions and a setting-independent measure.
    The published companion paper~\cite{Beau2026b} claims the contrary implication,
    but its proof restricts the Bell variable to observable-level data and its central
    conclusion is therefore not available as an input here.
    The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026l} proves that a projection is necessarily non-injective
    when it satisfies three premises (surjectivity, observables defined by the projection, effective
    non-triviality); Remark~\ref{rem:a2-status} explains why these premises are not established here
    for the local map of A2.
    The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026fe} develops the information-theoretic
    architecture of the resulting projection loss:
    it establishes a monotone fibre-erasure functional $I(c;\sigma(\ell))$
    non-increasing under admissible coarse-graining,
    connecting the ENI projection entropy to the BFS capacity hierarchy
    of the spectral programme.
    The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026q1} proves an exact, unconditional Fourier-support
    rigidity theorem in the finite Weil representation of the Heisenberg group, and the
    precise class of rank- and support-based diagnostics this forecloses; it does not
    derive phase coherence, the singlet correlator, the Tsirelson bound, or the Born
    rule from admissibility.
    The companion papers~\cite{Beau2026entnote,Beau2026ent1,Beau2026ent2} locate entanglement
    quantitatively within the spectral admissibility cascade:
    conditional on two explicit, undemonstrated hypotheses -- the atomic-carrier hypothesis
    (H-carrier) of~\cite{Beau2026ent2}, whose rank-three input is the supplied selection rule
    of~\cite{Beau2026a27} within the open fibre-identification problem of~\cite{Beau2026a22},
    and a diagonal-invariance hypothesis (\cite{Beau2026q3}) -- the minimal
    admissible pair sector carries the spin-$\tfrac12$ singlet with entropy $\log 2$, while the
    full residual fibre-level rank gives a finer observable $S_{\mathrm{ent}}(n)=\log r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$.
    The white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} develops the full phenomenological programme;
    the present axiomatic reformulation is a foundational companion to it, not yet
    integrated into its main text.

  \section{The Four Axioms}
  \label{sec:axioms}

  We state the four axioms from which the framework proceeds.
  They are not arbitrary postulates chosen for convenience — each captures
  a structural feature motivated either by the necessity theorem for emergent
  projections~\cite{Beau2026l}, whose premises are not established here for the local map of A2
  (Remark~\ref{rem:a2-status}), or by the requirement of bounded effective descriptions~\cite{Beau2026c}.

  \begin{definition}[Admissible transition]
    \label{def:admissible-transition}
    A transition from observable state $O_{n-1}$ to observable state $O_n$ is
    \emph{admissible} if it satisfies Axioms A1--A4 below.
  \end{definition}

  \begin{remark}[Admissibility is relational, not absolute]
    \label{rem:admissibility-relational}
    Admissibility is never an intrinsic predicate of an observable state $O_n$ taken in
    isolation.
    It is a relational constraint on projective transitions: a state $O_n$ is admissible
    only relative to a preceding state $O_{n-1}$, whose accumulated constraints determine
    the local admissible neighbourhood.
    We write $O_n \in \mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$ to make this explicit, where
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:admissible-neighbourhood}
      \mathcal{A}(O_{n-1}) \;:=\;
      \bigl\{\, O_n \mid \text{the transition } O_{n-1} \to O_n \text{ satisfies A1--A4} \,\bigr\}
    \end{equation}
    is the \emph{admissible neighbourhood from $O_{n-1}$}.
    The phrasing ``$O_n$ is admissible'' should always be read as shorthand for
    $O_n \in \mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$: admissibility is always \emph{from-here} admissibility,
    conditioned on the already-realized projective position.
    There is no fixed space of possible observable states: there is a field of local
    admissible neighbourhoods, each determined by the point already reached.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{description}
    \item[\textbf{A1 — Local projective admissibility.}]
    For any observable state $O_{n-1}$, there exists a non-empty set $F_n$ of
    admissible successor directions: configurations of $O_{n-1}$ compatible with
    a transition to a stable next state.
    The set $F_n$ is defined by internal structural constraints
    (Born--Infeld bound, spectral envelope, symmetry requirements)
    and does not presuppose an ambient configuration space.

    \item[\textbf{A2 — Structural non-injectivity.}]
    Distinct admissible directions from $O_{n-1}$ may lead to the same resolved
    successor state $O_n$.
    The map $\Pi_n$ encoding this is generically non-injective.

    \item[\textbf{A3 — Admissibility as non-premature selection.}]
    An admissible transition preserves the full multiplicity of open directions
    until projection locking occurs.
    It does not select among them prematurely — that is, before the
    capacity saturation threshold of~\cite{Beau2026c} is reached.

    \item[\textbf{A4 — Projection locking.}]
    Resolution occurs only when continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the
    discrete support of the observable image of the projection.
    In the spectral admissibility realisation, this support is the shell
    decomposition of the observable space~\cite{Beau2026a26}.
    The transition from admissible to admitted is structurally discrete.
  \end{description}

  \begin{remark}[Roles of the axioms, and the status of A2]
  \label{rem:a2-status}
  A1 asserts existence of admissible directions.
  A2 asserts their generic non-injectivity.
  A3 constrains which transitions are admissible.
  A4 governs when resolution occurs.
  These are distinct roles; no logical-independence theorem is claimed for A1--A4.
  ENI~\cite{Beau2026l} proves that a projection is necessarily non-injective whenever it is surjective, its
  observables are defined by the projection, and the description is effectively non-trivial.
  Importing that theorem requires naming the local map it would apply to, and the fibre map is not it:
  $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ by definition, so the restriction of $\Pi_n$ to $F_n$ is constant and satisfies no
  effective non-triviality condition.
  The candidate is instead a local transition map
  \[
    \widehat{\Pi}_n \colon \Omega_n^{\mathrm{loc}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O}_n^{\mathrm{loc}},
  \]
  where $\Omega_n^{\mathrm{loc}}$ collects the admissible directions available from $O_{n-1}$ and
  $\mathcal{O}_n^{\mathrm{loc}} \subseteq \mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$ collects the resolved successors they reach;
  A2 is the assertion that this map is generically many-to-one.
  The three ENI premises are not established for $\widehat{\Pi}_n$ here.
  Surjectivity onto $\mathcal{O}_n^{\mathrm{loc}}$ holds by construction, but that observables at step $n$ are
  defined by $\widehat{\Pi}_n$ alone, and that the local description is effectively non-trivial, are separate
  conditions on the observable level, not consequences of A1.
  Until a lemma supplies them, A2 is retained as a local axiom, and its ``generic'' formulation is kept.
  Establishing the lemma would replace A2 by a necessary consequence of ENI and require a re-audit of the
  downstream statements written under it.
  The joint role of the four axioms is argued in Section~\ref{sec:primitive} and in the
  companion papers~\cite{Beau2026l,Beau2026c}.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Status of Born--Infeld in A3 and A4]
  The saturation threshold appearing in A3 and A4 is the capacity bound stated as
  the explicit capacity axiom [A-cap] of the companion paper~\cite{Beau2026c},
  motivated there by non-injective projection with finite local distinguishability.
  The Born--Infeld form used in this paper is the saturation candidate compatible
  with that axiom; its unique selection would require an exceptional-propagation
  condition (\`a la Boillat--Pleba\'nski) whose derivation from projection
  structure remains open.
  A3 and A4 therefore rest on A1--A2 together with the capacity axiom
  of~\cite{Beau2026c}, with the Born--Infeld saturation form as an explicit
  additional input.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[What projection locking selects]
    \label{rem:a4-selects}
    A4 resolves the transition to a unique observable state~$O_n$ in the image of~$\Pi_n$.
    It does not select a unique underlying configuration in~$F_n$.

    The multiplicity of admissible configurations in the fibre $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ is
    not eliminated by projection locking: distinct configurations $f, f' \in F_n$ with
    $\Pi_n(f) = \Pi_n(f') = O_n$ remain structurally present after locking, but become
    observationally inaccessible.
    Where $\Pi_n$ is non-injective, that non-injectivity therefore persists through resolution:
    locking withdraws observational access, it does not remove the configurations.

    This distinction --- observable uniqueness without configurational uniqueness --- does not
    by itself prevent Bell-type factorisation.
    Any Bell-local model can be made non-injective by adjoining an independent erased label,
    without changing its observable probabilities.
    A separate obstruction to a setting-independent global coupling would be required.
    More generally, distinct interactions $I_1 \neq I_2$ induce distinct admissible
    sub-fibres $F_n^{(I_1)} \neq F_n^{(I_2)}$ and hence distinct observable outcomes,
    without any element of $F_n$ having been selected.
    This is the structural origin of contextuality: measurement context determines
    which sub-fibre is accessed, not which configuration is realised.
    This contextuality is \emph{synchronic}: it concerns the choice of sub-fibre at a
    fixed transition step $n$.
    The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$ (Remark~\ref{rem:admissibility-relational})
    introduces a second, \emph{diachronic} form of contextuality: the set of accessible
    observable states at step $n$ is itself conditioned on the previously realized state
    $O_{n-1}$.
    The context of a projection is therefore not merely the measurement algebra chosen at
    step $n$, but also the already-realized projective position from which the next state
    may be reached.
    Standard contextuality frameworks (Kochen--Specker, sheaf-theoretic) capture synchronic
    contextuality; the present framework extends this to diachronic contextuality, in which
    the history of realized projections is constitutive of what is admissible next.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Projective Observability Principle]
    \label{rem:projective-observability}
    Axioms A1--A4 jointly imply the following criterion, which identifies what is
    physical in the framework.

    \emph{Physical observables coincide with the invariants of the projection~$\Pi_n$:
    any quantity that depends on the choice of pre-image within~$\Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$
      is unphysical by construction.}

    This is not an additional axiom but a consequence of A2 (which forces non-trivial
    fibres) and A4 (which identifies $O_n$ with $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_n$ exactly):
    since $\Pi_n$ is the only structure that relates the configurational level to the
    observable level, any quantity invisible to $\Pi_n$ is invisible to physics.
    What the projection erases --- intra-fibre distinctions --- is unphysical;
    what it preserves --- the invariants of the fibre structure --- constitutes the
    physical content of the theory.
    In particular, the symmetry group of a fibre $\Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ is physical precisely
    because it acts within the fibre while leaving $O_n$ unchanged: this is the structural
    origin of gauge symmetry, developed in the companion paper~\cite{Beau2026q6a}, which defines gauge charges as
    fibre invariants and does not determine the gauge group.
    A detailed formulation in terms of the five-level hierarchy is given in
    Remark~\ref{rem:observable-space}.
  \end{remark}

  \section{The Primitive and the Role of $\Pi$}
  \label{sec:primitive}

  \subsection{The primitive}

    The only primitive of this framework is the pair $(O_{n-1}, F_n)$: an observable
    state and the set of admissible directions it admits.
    There is no external substrate, no hidden carrier, no background geometry.
    The fibre $F_n$ is not defined in an abstract ambient space — it is induced by
    $O_{n-1}$ itself through the admissibility constraints of A1--A3.

    The resulting evolution is recursive: once $O_n$ is realized, it immediately becomes
    the new departure point, generating the next admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(O_n)$
    from which $O_{n+1}$ may be reached:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:recursive-chain}
      O_{n-1}
      \;\longrightarrow\; O_n
      \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{A}(O_n)
      \;\longrightarrow\; O_{n+1}.
    \end{equation}
    The realized state is simultaneously a destination and the generator of the next set of
    constraints.
    No external agent resets the context between steps: the admissible neighbourhood at
    each stage is determined entirely by the state just reached.

  \subsection{The projection $\Pi_n$ as descriptive abstraction}

    The notation $\Pi_n$ is a compact encoding of the local admissibility structure.
    It encodes three things simultaneously:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item the admissible directions from $O_{n-1}$ — the fibre $F_n$ (A1, A2);
\item the Born--Infeld saturation bound — the limit on accessible amplitudes (A3);
\item projection locking — the constraint that resolution falls on the available
discrete support (A4).
    \end{enumerate}

    $\Pi_n$ is not a physical entity and not a dynamical map.
    It is a structural relation encoding the admissible identification between
    distinguishable configurations (elements of $F_n$) and indistinguishable
    observable outcomes (elements of $O_n$).
    Saying ``$\Pi_n$ is non-injective'' means that multiple directions from
    $O_{n-1}$ can lead to the same $O_n$ — a fact about the admissible transition
    structure, not a property of an external map.
    Non-injectivity does not originate from an underlying substrate, but from
    the structure of admissible configurations and their interaction-dependent
    realisation map.

    \begin{remark}[The threefold role of $\Pi_n$]
      \label{rem:pi-threefold}
      The structural consequences established below in Sections~\ref{sec:ni-consequences}
      and~\ref{subsec:projective-incompleteness} reveal that $\Pi_n$ plays three
      distinct roles simultaneously, each irreducible to the others.
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item \emph{Admissibility filter.}
        $\Pi_n$ selects, from the structurally compatible continuations
        $\Omega_n^{(c)}$, those directions satisfying the Born--Infeld and spectral
        constraints of A1--A3.
        What it retains constitutes the observable $O_n$; where the step is non-injective, which
        A2 asserts to be the generic case, what it discards is the non-trivial kernel whose
        existence is guaranteed by Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness}.
        \item \emph{Generator of temporal order, conditionally.}
        Under \textup{[H-acyc]} (Definition~\ref{hyp:acyclic}), which is not derived here, the
        oriented sequence $\Pi_0, \Pi_1, \Pi_2, \ldots$ carries the partial order of
        Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-order}.
        Each $\Pi_n$ is a node in the admissibility graph
        (Remark~\ref{rem:state-machine}); on that hypothesis the index $n$ acquires its temporal
        meaning from this orientation, not from an external parameter, and the dynamics of the
        framework is the oriented topology of this graph rather than a law superimposed on a
        pre-existing time.
        Without \textup{[H-acyc]} the sequence is still a chain of steps, each losing information
        in its immediate image, but it is not shown to be acyclic.
        \item \emph{Revelation, not creation.}
        $\Pi_n$ does not produce new structure in $\chi$.
        In the supplied O-series realisation, it selects from the admissible
        spectral modes carried by $\mathcal{H}_c$ those that are observationally
        accessible at depth $n$.
        The BFS depth controls how much of the structure of $\chi$ has been
        revealed; increasing $n$ does not modify $\chi$ but resolves finer spectral
        content — as a deeper lens resolves detail that was always present in the
        object, not detail that the lens creates.
      \end{enumerate}
      These three roles are inseparable: the structural constraints A1--A3 and
      Born--Infeld parity that force the admissibility filter simultaneously
      generate the temporal order via Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-order}, conditionally
      on \textup{[H-acyc]}, and
      guarantee, for the non-injective transitions A2 asserts to be generic, the
      incompleteness of the resolution via Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness}.
      These roles do not by themselves select the carrier group.
      The carrier-selection boundary is stated in
      Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg}.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The index $n$}

    The index $n$ does not represent a pre-existing time parameter.
    It counts transitions: the number of times a state has been succeeded by
    another under the admissibility constraints.
    Its temporal meaning is derived in Section~\ref{sec:ni-consequences} rather than assumed,
    but that derivation is conditional: orienting the chain requires \textup{[H-acyc]}
    (Definition~\ref{hyp:acyclic}), which is not established here.
    Without it, $n$ counts transitions without being shown to be irreversible along the chain.

  \subsection{Where non-injectivity lives}
    \label{subsec:ni-locus}

    A natural question arises: where does the non-injectivity of $\Pi_n$ come from,
    given that no underlying substrate is postulated?
    The answer is precise.

    Non-injectivity is a property of the interaction-dependent realisation map:
    the map from the space $F_n$ of structurally compatible configurations to the space
    $O_n$ of observable outcomes.
    When two distinct admissible configurations $f, f' \in F_n$ give rise to
    the same observable outcome $o \in O_n$, it is not because they are
    ``the same at a deeper level'' — it is because the interaction that
    realises the transition cannot distinguish them within $O_n$.
    The limit on distinguishability is set by the Born--Infeld capacity
    constraint (A3): the observable space $O_n$ has finite projective capacity,
    and configurations that differ only below this threshold are identified.

    Three properties of this realisation map are essential:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \emph{Interaction-dependence}: $\Pi_n$ encodes the interaction;
different interactions define different realisation maps and different
fibres $F_n$.
\item \emph{Internal definition of admissibles}: $F_n$ is defined by
internal constraints (BI bound, spectral envelope, symmetry) — not by
reference to an ambient substrate.
\item \emph{Minimal structure of $F_n$}: the admissibility constraints
restrict the algebraic structure $F_n$ can carry but do not select it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg}); the SU(2) structure of the spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$
sector enters through the spinor carrier supplied conditionally by
O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}, whose selection is open.
    \end{enumerate}

    The non-injectivity is therefore neither a property of a hidden substrate
    nor an arbitrary assumption: it is a structural consequence of finite
    projective capacity applied to an internally defined space of admissible
    configurations.

    \begin{remark}[The admissible fibre as equivalence class under projection]
      \label{rem:shadow-analogy}
      A useful image for the non-injectivity of~$\Pi_n$ is that of shadows cast by a fixed
      projection setup.
      A given shadow does not correspond to a single object:
      it corresponds to the set of all objects whose projection yields that shadow ---
      the admissible fibre $F_n = \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$.

      The directionality is essential.
      The fibre is not the collection of all shadows produced by a given object under varying
      projections (one configuration, many images).
      It is the collection of all configurations that produce the same observable under a fixed,
      non-injective~$\Pi_n$ (many configurations, one observable).
      This many-to-one structure is the source of the immediate information loss
      (Section~\ref{sec:ni-consequences}) and of the non-factorisability of the proto-state
      (Section~\ref{subsec:heisenberg}).
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Conditional connection to the O-series carrier]
      \label{rem:fn-vrho}
      The O-series supplies the Cayley graph
      $\mathrm{Cay}(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}),S_q)$ and a
      Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$.
      Its spectral construction then uses the associated symplectic action: the Weil representation of
      $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ on the carrier space, which normalises the Heisenberg action.
      We write $\mathcal{W}$ for the resulting finite Weil modules, one for each non-trivial central character:
      the member at $c$ is the Weil block $V_c \simeq \mathbb{C}^q$ of~\cite{Beau2026a31}, the carrier space of
      $\mathcal{H}_c$ with its Weil action, and $V_{q-c} \simeq \overline{V_c}$.
      The Weil action on $V_c$ is not the Heisenberg action of $\mathcal{H}_c$, and the notation identifies
      neither $\mathcal{W}$ nor $V_c$ with $F_n$ or with the spinor carrier $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ supplied
      conditionally by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
      Identifying the abstract admissible fibre with the supplied Heisenberg
      carrier is a modelling dictionary,
      \begin{equation}
        F_n \;\simeq\; \mathcal{H}_c
        \qquad\text{(conditional O-series realisation),}
      \end{equation}
      not a consequence of A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity.
      Once a finite Heisenberg group and a non-trivial central character are
      supplied, Stone--von Neumann fixes $\mathcal{H}_c$ up to unitary
      equivalence.
      It does not supply the group, and it does not identify the Heisenberg action
      with the distinct Weil action; Section~\ref{subsec:heisenberg} proves this
      carrier-selection obstruction.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The two levels of $\Pi_n$: kernel and image (O24)]
      \label{rem:pi-two-levels}
      The non-injective projection $\Pi_n$ operates at two structurally distinct
      levels, established in O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}:
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \emph{Kernel level} ($\ker \Pi_n$): governs microscopic multiplicity
  and fluctuation amplitude.
  Multiple configurations in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ may project to the same
  observable outcome; their differences are invisible to admissible
  observables and appear as projection residuals.
  The dependence on $O_{n-1}$ — via the baseline $V_{n-1}^{(c)}$ — enters
  at this level: it determines which directions in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ are
  new relative to the already realized subspace.
  \item \emph{Image level} ($\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_n \cap \mathrm{Ntrl}$):
  governs the observable structure.
  Its real rank is taken to be 3, the adjoint dimension of the supplied
  spinor carrier (O23, Theorem~3.1) — a supplied selection rule whose
  derivation from admissibility is open — and is independent of the
  cardinality of $\ker \Pi_n$.
      \end{enumerate}
      The Verticality Lemma of O24 (Lemma~3.1) establishes that every
      Born--Infeld-admissible symmetry acts vertically: it maps each fibre
      $\Pi_n^{-1}(o)$ to itself and does not increase the rank of the image.
      The structural consequence is:
      \begin{quote}
        \emph{Non-injectivity affects degeneracy, not structure.}
      \end{quote}
      Enlarging $\ker \Pi_n$ — admitting more configurations in each fibre —
      increases the amplitude of quantum fluctuations but does not alter the
      observable rank, the cascade exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$, or any
      other structural invariant of the admissible sector.
      The two levels of $\Pi_n$ are therefore decoupled: kernel structure is
      physically relevant for fluctuations; image structure is physically
      relevant for observables.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The observable space principle: $\mathcal{O} = \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$]
      \label{rem:observable-space}
      A direct consequence of the five-level hierarchy is that admissible
      observables are functions on
      \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{O} \;:=\; \mathrm{Im}\,\Pi
        \;=\; \bigsqcup_{n} O_n,
      \end{equation}
      not on the pre-image space~$\Omega_n^{(c)}$.
      Any structural difference between two configurations
      $f, f' \in \Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ that share the same image under~$\Pi_n$
      is invisible to every admissible observable by definition:
      it is a difference \emph{within} the fibre, which the projection
      identifies as observationally indistinguishable.
      This is not an additional assumption but an immediate consequence of
      Definition~\ref{def:omega-n} and Level~5:
      $O_n$ is defined as $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi_n$, and admissibility is
      a property of $\Pi_n$ acting on $\Omega_n^{(c)}$, not of
      $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ itself.
      Intra-fibre variability is therefore structurally unobservable; it
      constitutes the degree of freedom whose projected manifestation appears
      as quantum indeterminacy in the effective description.
      In particular, any admissible quantity that depends on the choice of
      representative in $\Pi_n^{-1}(O_n)$ must agree for all representatives
      in the same fibre class: this is the structural basis for the uniformity
      of admissible spectral invariants across the fibre.
      The information-theoretic quantification of this erasure ---
      as a monotone non-increasing functional under admissible coarse-graining
      --- is developed in~\cite{Beau2026fe}.
    \end{remark}


    \label{subsec:five-levels}

    The abstract structure of Section~\ref{sec:primitive} has a concrete realisation
    in the O-series that answers precisely where $\Omega_n$ lives and how it is
    constructed.
    The realisation proceeds through five levels.
    Its first carrier choice is supplied rather than induced by A1--A4.

    \paragraph{Level 1 — The supplied relational group $G_q$.}
      The O-series chooses the discrete Heisenberg group
      \begin{equation}
        G_q \;=\; \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}),
        \qquad |G_q| = q^3,
      \end{equation}
      equipped with the standard symmetric generating set $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$.
      $G_q$ is not a physical space: it is a relational structure, encoding how
      configurations relate to one another via the generators.
      The BFS explores $G_q$ in shells $S_n = B_n \setminus B_{n-1}$, where
      $B_n = \{g \in G_q : d(e,g) \leq n\}$.
      The shell structure $\{S_n\}$ is the concrete realisation of the
      transition-counting index $n$ of Section~\ref{sec:primitive}.

    \paragraph{Level 2 — The Heisenberg representation $\pi_c$ and its carrier.}
      For each non-zero character $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the
      discrete Heisenberg group $G_q$ admits an irreducible $q$-dimensional
      Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger representation~\cite{Beau2026a16}:
      \begin{equation}
        \pi_c : G_q \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{H}_c),
        \qquad
        (\pi_c(a,b,\gamma)\,f)(x) \;=\;
        e^{2\pi i c(\gamma + bx)/q}\, f(x-a),
      \end{equation}
      where $\mathcal{H}_c=L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{C}^q$.
      This is the Heisenberg action, not the Weil action.
      Given the supplied pair $(G_q,c)$, Stone--von Neumann fixes its carrier up
      to unitary equivalence.

    \paragraph{Level 3 — The orbit span $\Omega_n$ as emerging subspace.}
      Given the initial vector $v_0 \in \mathcal{H}_c$ and the Heisenberg action
      $\pi_c$,
      the orbit span at BFS depth $n$ is:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:omega-n}
        \Omega_n^{(c)}
        \;:=\;
        \mathrm{span}\bigl\{\pi_c(g)\,v_0 : g \in B_n\bigr\}
        \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{H}_c.
      \end{equation}
      By construction, $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is a minimal extension of the previously
      realized subspace:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:omega-n-inclusion}
        V_{n-1}^{(c)} \;\subseteq\; \Omega_n^{(c)} \;\subseteq\; \mathbb{C}^q,
      \end{equation}
      where $V_{n-1}^{(c)} = \mathrm{GS}(B_{n-1})$ is the Gram--Schmidt span
      accumulated over the preceding shells.
      $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is not defined by $O_{n-1}$, but is constrained by the
      already realized subspace $V_{n-1}^{(c)}$: the directions in
      $\Omega_n^{(c)} \setminus V_{n-1}^{(c)}$ are the candidates for new
      observable structure at depth $n$.

      \begin{definition}[Structurally compatible continuations]
        \label{def:omega-n}
        $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is the space of \emph{structurally compatible continuations}
        from $O_{n-1}$: it contains every direction reachable by the relational
        structure of $G_q$ via $\pi_c$, without any admissibility filter applied.
        Admissibility is \emph{not} a property of $\Omega_n^{(c)}$;
        it is a property of $\Pi_n$ acting on $\Omega_n^{(c)}$.
      \end{definition}

      Three properties follow immediately:
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \emph{Emergence}: $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is not given a priori — it is built
incrementally as the BFS explores $G_q$.
\item \emph{Indirect conditioning on $O_{n-1}$}:
$\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is defined by the group action alone — the definition
\eqref{eq:omega-n} refers only to $B_n$ and $\pi_c$.
The dependence on $O_{n-1}$ is indirect: $O_{n-1}$ determines the
Gram--Schmidt span $\mathrm{GS}_{n-1}$ already accumulated over $B_{n-1}$,
which acts as the baseline against which new directions in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$
are measured.
This baseline is used by $\Pi_n$ (not by $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ itself) to
identify genuinely new directions, counted by $\sigma_c(n) = \delta r_n / |S_n|$~\cite{Beau2026a16}.
In summary: $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is determined by the supplied data
$(G_q,\pi_c,B_n)$;
the role of $O_{n-1}$ enters only through $\Pi_n$.
\item \emph{Pre-admissibility character}:
$\Omega_n^{(c)}$ contains all directions generated by the group action
of $G_q$ up to depth $n$ — including directions that $\Pi_n$ will
subsequently reject as inadmissible.
The admissibility filter is entirely in $\Pi_n$, not in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$.
      \end{enumerate}

    \paragraph{Level 4 — The admissibility filter $\Pi_n$.}
      The projection $\Pi_n$ acts on $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ via the BI admissibility
      constraint $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ and the Gram--Schmidt
      orthogonalisation~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a26}.
      It selects, from all directions in $\Omega_n^{(c)}$, those compatible with
      the admissibility constraints.
      $\Pi_n$ encodes the laws revealed by the O-series — Born--Infeld bound,
      candidate parity involution, projection locking — but does not define $\Omega_n^{(c)}$.

    \paragraph{Level 5 — The observable outcome $O_n$.}
      The observable state $O_n$ is the image of $\Pi_n$ applied to the admissible
      part of $\Omega_n^{(c)}$.
      It is a discrete, shell-indexed element of the observable
      space~\cite{Beau2026a26}:
      \begin{equation}
        O \;=\; \bigsqcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}_q} O_n.
      \end{equation}

      \begin{remark}[Summary: the five-level hierarchy]
  Conditional on the supplied carrier, the five levels constitute the
  following construction:
  \begin{equation}
    G_q
    \;\xrightarrow{\pi_c}\;
    \mathcal{H}_c
    \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{BFS}}\;
    \Omega_n^{(c)}
    \;\xrightarrow{\Pi_n}\;
    O_n,
    \qquad
    V_{n-1}^{(c)} \subseteq \Omega_n^{(c)} \subseteq \mathcal{H}_c.
  \end{equation}
  $G_q$ is the relational structure.
  $\mathcal{H}_c$ is its Heisenberg representation carrier.
  $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ is the emergent space of structurally compatible continuations
  (not yet filtered by admissibility),
  constrained from below by the already realized subspace $V_{n-1}^{(c)}$.
  $\Pi_n$ is the admissibility filter.
  $O_n$ is the resolved observable state.
  No level presupposes the next: each is induced by the preceding structure
  and the admissibility constraints.
  This is the concrete answer to the question of where $\Omega_n$ lives:
  in $\mathbb{C}^q$, itself induced by $G_q$ — not in an external configuration
  space.
      \end{remark}

      \begin{remark}[The conjugate pair and $\Omega_n$ without preferred direction]
  The conjugation $c \leftrightarrow q-c$ acts on $\Omega_n^{(c)}$
  via complex conjugation~\cite{Beau2026a21}:
  $\Omega_n^{(q-c)} = \overline{\Omega_n^{(c)}}$.
  Reading this pair as the minimal admissible fibre is the candidate parity
  structure of the programme (a structurally motivated hypothesis; the fibre
  identification is stated as an open classification problem
  in~\cite{Beau2026a22}); under that reading the minimal fibre is the pair
  $\{V_n^{(c)}, V_n^{(q-c)}\} = \{V_n^{(c)}, \overline{V_n^{(c)}}\}$,
  with no preferred orientation — consistent with the admissibility principle
  of A3.
      \end{remark}

  \subsection{From $\mathbb{C}^q$ to continuous spacetime: the large-$q$ limit}
  \label{subsec:continuum-limit}

  The five-level hierarchy of Section~\ref{subsec:five-levels} is formulated
  for finite prime $q$.
  Physical spacetime is continuous.
  This subsection identifies the structural bridge between the two, without
  claiming to complete the derivation.

  \paragraph{The discrete structure at finite $q$.}
    At finite $q$, the observable space $O = \bigsqcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}_q} O_n$
    is discrete and finite.
    The ambient representation space $\mathbb{C}^q$ has dimension $q$.
    Physical quantities are functions on $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$.

  \paragraph{The large-$q$ limit.}
    As $q \to \infty$ with the prime structure retained, a continuum description would require
    three things simultaneously:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \emph{A continuum carrier limit would require an explicit scaling map}.
The finite Heisenberg carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$ and its associated Weil module
must be kept distinct.
No convergence from either finite action to an operator on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$
follows from the carrier construction alone; the normalization no-go discussed
below is an obstruction to such a bridge.
\item \emph{The BFS shell structure would have to approach a continuous foliation}.
For the discrete Heisenberg group, $c_1 n^4 \le |B_n| \le c_2 n^4$ (Bass--Guivarc'h~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973},
homogeneous dimension $D = 4$), with the asymptotic $|B_n| \sim C n^4$ due to Pansu~\cite{Pansu1983}; the supplied
finite carrier $G_q$ has this growth only on balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt{q}$ (Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
A continuous layering of the configuration
space compatible with a four-dimensional effective geometry~\cite{Cosmochrony} would
follow only from a spatial continuum limit, which is open.
\item \emph{The discrete admissibility filter $\Pi_n$ would have to approach a
differential constraint}.
The capacity admissibility bound $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$
would become, in such a limit, the capacity constraint on effective field
configurations~\cite{Beau2026c}, whose Born--Infeld saturation candidate
provides the effective Lagrangian description.
    \end{enumerate}

    If these three requirements were met, the continuum spacetime observable would not be a
    separate postulate: it would be the large-$q$ limit of the discrete observable space $O$,
    with the four-dimensional volume growth of the supplied Heisenberg carrier, which $G_q$ reproduces only on
    balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt{q}$.
    That limit is not established (Remark~\ref{rem:continuum-limit}).

    \begin{remark}[Status of the continuum limit]
      \label{rem:continuum-limit}
      The convergence $\mathbb{C}^q \to L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and the emergence of
      continuous spacetime geometry from the BFS shell structure are described
      qualitatively in~\cite{Cosmochrony}; they are not established.
      The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026q5a} identifies the canonical
      filtration exactly (with the pipeline's initial vector, the filtration stage is
      precisely a growing toric Fourier window), proves that the published
      admissibility form converges to the zero form on this filtration, and proves
      that, under its stated conditions on the saturation depth and the weights, no common scalar
      normalisation of the form produces a non-trivial toric differential operator.
      The existence of a spatial limit operator is the explicit, unestablished
      hypothesis [H-L]; the identification of the effective metric
      from the symbol of $L_\Pi$ (Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}) is conditional on [H-L] and on the open
      lifting hypothesis [H-lift].
      The present remark identifies the intended mechanism; its rigorous realisation
      is the open content of Q5.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Why four dimensions]
  For the discrete Heisenberg group with its two horizontal generators, the central direction is
  generated by their commutator and carries weight~$2$, so the Bass--Guivarc'h formula
  $D = \sum_k k\,\mathrm{rank}(\gamma_k/\gamma_{k+1})$, where $(\gamma_k)$ is the lower central series and
  $\mathrm{rank}$ the torsion-free rank, gives
  $D = 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 1 = 4$~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}.
  The finite group $G_q$ has this growth only locally, on balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt{q}$
  (Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
  The value $D = 4$ is a property of the supplied Heisenberg carrier, which the axioms do not select
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg}); it is not adjusted to the observed dimension, and it is not derived
  from A1--A4.
  It is the homogeneous dimension of the spatial factor alone: Q5b keeps it distinct from the topological
  dimension of $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$, and no result identifies it with the
  $3+1$ structure of observed spacetime.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Structural Consequences of Non-Injectivity}
  \label{sec:ni-consequences}

  Non-injectivity (A2) and the existence of distinct successor states together
  imply three structural facts whose sources are distinct.

  \subsection{Immediate information loss}

    \begin{proposition}[Immediate information loss from non-injectivity]
      \label{prop:irreversibility}
      Every admissible transition whose realisation map is non-injective, which A2 asserts
      to be the generic case, loses structural information in its immediate image: the
      directions it identifies are not distinguishable in $O_n$, and $\Pi_n$ admits no
      inverse.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Since multiple directions from $O_{n-1}$ map to the same $O_n$ under
      $\Pi_n$, the information distinguishing these directions is absent from
      $O_n$, and $\Pi_n$ has positive projection entropy
      $S_{\Pi_n} > 0$~\cite{Beau2026l}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Permanence is not established here]
      \label{rem:no-permanence}
      The proposition is local to the step $n$.
      That no later transition can recover the identified directions does not follow from it:
      $S_\Pi \geq 0$ bounds each projection separately and constrains no composition, so
      non-negativity is not a monotonicity statement along $n$.
      Fibre Erasure~\cite{Beau2026fe} is explicit that ENI alone does not imply monotonicity;
      its monotone requires the structural sufficiency hypothesis \textup{[H-suff]} and runs
      along the coarse-graining axis $\ell$, which is not the temporal axis $n$.
      Permanent non-recovery would need a temporal sufficiency or Markov property that is not
      derived here.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Novelty and structural fluctuations}
    \label{subsec:ni-novelty}

    The existence of a distinct successor state $O_n \neq O_{n-1}$ defines a
    structural residue:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:delta-n}
      \Delta_n \;:=\; \mathrm{Res}(O_{n-1}, O_n;\, \Pi_n),
    \end{equation}
    the measure of how much $O_n$ is irreducible to a continuation of $O_{n-1}$
    under $\Pi_n$.
    That $\Delta_n$ is non-trivial is not an additional assumption — it is the
    condition under which we index $O_n$ as a distinct state.
    These residues are what physical descriptions call fluctuations or quantum noise.

  \subsection{Temporal order}

    Non-invertibility of each step does not by itself orient the states.
    A segment may have every transition non-injective and still revisit a state: let
    $O_0 \to O_1$ and $O_1 \to O_0$ be carried by two different non-injective maps.
    Each step loses information in its immediate image, yet the reachability graph has a cycle and
    admits no strict partial order.
    Novelty between adjacent states does not exclude a later return either.
    Orienting the chain therefore requires an input beyond A2.

    \begin{definition}[{[H-acyc]}: no return; not established]
      \label{hyp:acyclic}
      The reachability relation generated by the admissible transitions is acyclic; equivalently,
      some rank function on observable states is strictly monotone along every admissible
      transition.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{theorem}[Temporal order; conditional on {[H-acyc]}]
      \label{thm:temporal-order}
      Along a segment of the chain of observable states $\{O_n\}$ whose transitions are all
      non-injective --- the generic case under A2 --- and under \textup{[H-acyc]}, the states carry
      a canonical oriented, non-trivial strict partial order.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Transitivity is given by composition of transitions.
      \textup{[H-acyc]} excludes cycles, hence gives irreflexivity and asymmetry of the
      reachability relation.
      Novelty ($\Delta_n \neq 0$) ensures $O_n \neq O_{n-1}$, so the order is non-trivial.
      Together these give a strict partial order compatible with the chain structure.
      Proposition~\ref{prop:irreversibility} supplies the information-theoretic content of each
      step, not the orientation.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What would establish {[H-acyc]}]
      \label{rem:acyclic-open}
      A strictly monotone rank on observable states, or a separate proof that no admissible cycle
      exists, would supply it.
      The temporal sufficiency property of Remark~\ref{rem:no-permanence} would not: a Markov
      property yields at best a non-increasing quantity by the data-processing inequality, which
      may stay constant, and a system may return to the same observable state without recovering
      the distinctions lost earlier.
      The two debts may well be closed by one stronger object, but they are not the same debt.
      Neither is derived here, so the arrow of time is a conditional consequence of this
      foundation, not a proved one.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Time as derived structure]
  The arrow of time is not postulated as a separate primitive.
  It is the oriented order carried by the chain of non-injective transitions under A2 once
  \textup{[H-acyc]} is granted; that hypothesis is not derived here.
  The index $n$ acquires its temporal meaning from this order — not the reverse.
  The quantitative structure of this projective temporal ordering — cumulative
  projected capacity and its numerical validation from the spectral admissibility
  cascade — is developed in~\cite{Beau2026pto}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Admissible transitions as a state machine]
      \label{rem:state-machine}
      The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$
      (Remark~\ref{rem:admissibility-relational}) gives the
      chain~\eqref{eq:recursive-chain} the structure of a directed state machine:
      nodes are admissible projections, edges are admissible transitions, and a
      physical history is a directed path in this graph.
      The graph is directed in all cases; that it is acyclic, and so carries an orientation in
      the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal-order}, is \textup{[H-acyc]} and is not
      established here.
      When $|\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})| = 1$, the successor is uniquely determined by the
      admissibility structure alone — no external law is required.
      When $|\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})| > 1$, branching occurs; the relative admissibility
      weights of the branches are the projective analogues of quantum probabilities
      (Section~\ref{sec:admissibility}).
      In both cases, no temporal parameter external to the transition structure is needed.
      Under \textup{[H-acyc]}, the index $n$ then acquires its temporal meaning entirely from the
      orientation of the admissibility graph, and \emph{dynamics is the oriented topology of that
      graph, not a law imposed upon a pre-existing time}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Observable reality as projective position]
      \label{rem:projective-position}
      The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$ is not a fixed space of
      possibilities.
      It shifts at every step, because $O_n$ conditions $\mathcal{A}(O_n)$, which differs
      generically from $\mathcal{A}(O_{n-1})$: the projection following step $n$ is never
      performed from an invariant reference frame.
      Observable reality is therefore not a static projection of a fixed substrate onto a
      fixed observable space.
      It is the \emph{current projective position within the space of admissible reprojections}:
      the trajectory $O_0 \leadsto O_1 \leadsto \cdots \leadsto O_n$ is constitutive of
      what is real at step $n$, not contingent upon it.
      The cumulative index $I(n)$ measures not elapsed time but accumulated projective
      divergence from the initial position — how far the sequence has moved through the
      space of admissible neighbourhoods.
      In particular, the projection does not reveal a pre-existing reality: it redefines the
      context from which the next reality may appear.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Projective incompleteness}
    \label{subsec:projective-incompleteness}

    Projective incompleteness is already encoded by structural non-injectivity.
    It must not be presented as a consequence of a selected Heisenberg carrier:
    Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg} proves that A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity do not
    select that carrier.

    \begin{corollary}[Projective incompleteness]
      \label{cor:projective-incompleteness}
      Under A2, an admissible non-injective realisation map $\Pi_n$ has a
      non-trivial fibre.
      When $\Pi_n$ is represented linearly, this gives
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:projective-incompleteness}
        \ker \Pi_n \;\neq\; \{0\}.
      \end{equation}
      Strict positivity of a chosen projection-entropy functional requires its
      own hypotheses.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      A2 states that distinct admissible configurations have the same observable
      image.
      For a linear realisation map their difference is a non-zero kernel element.
      No commutator or carrier-selection theorem is needed.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Structural fluctuations as necessary, not contingent]
      \label{rem:fluctuations-necessary}
      Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness} changes the epistemic status of the
      structural fluctuations introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:ni-novelty}.
      The non-trivial fibre follows from A2.
      Turning this multiplicity into a quantitative fluctuation amplitude requires a
      specified measure or projection-entropy functional and is not supplied by a
      commutator argument.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Projective incompleteness and the initial transition: scope]
      \label{rem:first-transition}
      Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness} does not settle what initiates $\Pi_1$ from $\Pi_0$.
      Being generic, A2 does not exclude that a particular transition, the initial one included,
      is injective; where $\Pi_1$ is non-injective the corollary applies to it.
      A2 does not by itself prove that an admissible successor exists, nor dynamically initiate
      the first transition.
      A1 posits a non-empty set of admissible directions from $O_0$; whether a transition along one of them
      also satisfies A3 and A4, so that $\mathcal{A}(O_0) \neq \emptyset$, is not shown here.
      Non-emptiness of $\mathcal{A}(O_0)$ is therefore a distinct condition.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Admissibility, Wave, and Phase Coherence}
  \label{sec:admissibility}

  \subsection{The admissibility principle}

    Axiom A3 introduces a physical constraint on transitions:

    \begin{quote}
      A transition is admissible if and only if it preserves the full multiplicity
      of open admissible directions without selecting among them prematurely.
    \end{quote}

    Premature selection means making a determinative choice that nothing in the
    current state $O_{n-1}$ justifies.
    Admissibility forbids it: the transition must remain structurally neutral among
    all open directions until projection locking (A4) forces resolution.

  \subsection{The proto-state}
    \label{subsec:proto-state}

    \begin{definition}[Proto-state]
      \label{def:proto-state}
      The \emph{proto-state} $\Psi_n$ associated with a transition from $O_{n-1}$
      to $O_n$ is the unresolved configuration shared between $O_{n-1}$ and $O_n$:
      the carrier of the transition, belonging fully to neither endpoint.
    \end{definition}

    The proto-state is physically real — it is the configuration of the system
    during the transition, before projection locking.
    It is not an epistemic representation of uncertainty about a definite state:
    it is the actual structural state of the system between two realized
    observable states.

    \begin{proposition}[Structural indeterminacy of the proto-state]
      \label{prop:indeterminacy}
      Under A1 and A3, the proto-state $\Psi_n$ is genuinely indeterminate among
      the admissible directions in $F_n$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By A1, $F_n$ is non-empty.
      By A3, the transition does not select prematurely among the elements of $F_n$.
      Therefore $\Psi_n$ is not identified with any particular element of $F_n$
      before projection locking: it is open across all of $F_n$ simultaneously.
      This is structural indeterminacy, not epistemic ignorance.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Wave, phase, and coherence}

    The proto-state, being structurally open across all elements of $F_n$
    simultaneously, propagates through all admissible directions at once.
    This simultaneous openness is the structural precursor of wave-like behaviour; interference also requires
    relative phases on a supplied complex carrier and their coherence, discussed next.

    Structural indeterminacy does not by itself supply relative phases between the open directions.
    Relative phase relations become representable once amplitudes are written on a complex carrier, and that
    carrier is supplied (Section~\ref{subsec:complex-amplitudes}).
    Their physical coherence is a further question.
    A3 forbids premature selection among the open directions, so it preserves their multiplicity.
    It does not by itself forbid an erasure of the relative phases that selects no direction: a configuration
    whose phases have been erased still keeps every direction open.
    Retention of phase coherence would follow from A3 only under the additional reading that such an erasure
    counts as premature selection; that reading is not derived here.
    The chain of consequences is therefore:

    \begin{equation}
      \begin{aligned}
        \text{A1, A3}
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{structural indeterminacy (proto-state)} \\
        \text{supplied complex carrier}
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{representable relative phases} \\
        \text{A3} + \text{additional reading}
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{coherence preserved.}
      \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    \begin{remark}[The admissible fibre as precursor of Hilbert structure]
  The fibre $F_n$, with its open directions preserved under A3, is the
  natural precursor of an effective Hilbert sector.
  Its complex amplitudes are those of the supplied carriers, the Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger carrier
  $\mathcal{H}_c$ and the spinor carrier supplied conditionally by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}; they are not a
  consequence of A3
  (Section~\ref{subsec:complex-amplitudes}).
  This is not yet a full Hilbert space — the inner product, completeness,
  and probability measure remain to be established; no companion paper in
  this programme currently supplies that derivation (see
  Section~\ref{sec:summary}).
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Double-slit experiment]
  In the double-slit experiment, the proto-state propagates through both slits
  because the admissibility constraints do not select between them:
  both are open directions in $F_n$.
  The interference pattern would follow from coherent propagation through all admissible directions, given
  relative phases on the supplied complex carrier and the additional reading of A3 under which they stay
  coherent.
  The impact on the screen is the moment the proto-state reaches the
  Born--Infeld saturation threshold — projection locking (A4) forces it onto
  an available shell, resolving it into a localized state $O_n$.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Non-factorisability and the carrier-selection boundary}
    \label{subsec:heisenberg}

    Axiom A3 can constrain a supplied representation of the admissible fibre,
    but it does not select a Heisenberg group.
    This subsection isolates the valid irreducibility consequence from the failed
    carrier-identification step.

    \begin{remark}[The structural tension between A2 and A4]
      \label{rem:a2-a4-tension}
      The non-factorisability established in this section can be understood as the minimal
      consistent resolution of a structural tension between A2 and A4.

      A2 asserts that the multiplicity of admissible configurations in~$F_n$ cannot be
      eliminated: distinct configurations lead to the same observable, and this
      indistinguishability is structural, not epistemic.
      A4 asserts that the observable outcome is unique at locking.
      Together, they impose an irresolvable constraint: the multiplicity cannot disappear
      (A2 would be violated), yet it cannot remain directly observable (A4 would be violated).

      A3 --- admissibility as non-premature selection --- is the operative axiom that
      determines how this tension resolves.
      It forbids any factorisation of~$F_n$ that would separate the multiplicity into invariant sectors
      prior to locking, forcing an irreducible structure.
      Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation} formalises this constraint once a linear
      representation dictionary is supplied.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg} shows why this does not identify the carrier
      group.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{lemma}[Admissible non-factorisability]
      \label{lem:non-factorisation}
      Under A1--A3 and a representation-theoretic dictionary for $F_n$, any decomposition
      \[
        F_n \;=\; F^{(1)} \oplus F^{(2)}
      \]
      that is stable under the admissible generators of $F_n$ is forbidden prior
      to projection locking.
      In particular, A3 forces the action of admissible generators on $F_n$ to
      be irreducible.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      A3 forbids premature selection among open directions.
      A decomposition of $F_n$ stable under admissible generators separates
      directions by invariants that are internal to the fibre — invariants carried
      by the generators themselves, which are constitutive of the admissibility
      structure by A1.
      Such a decomposition therefore resolves the proto-state partially, by
      assigning each open direction to a definite sector, prior to any
      Born--Infeld saturation event.
      This contradicts A3.
      Irreducibility follows immediately: any proper stable sub-representation
      would yield such a forbidden decomposition.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{theorem}[Obstruction to Heisenberg carrier selection]
      \label{thm:heisenberg}
      Minimal generation by a pair $X,Y$, an involution exchanging that pair,
      and a faithful irreducible unitary representation of dimension greater
      than one do not imply that $[X,Y]$ is central.
      Consequently, the carrier contract extracted from A1--A3 and
      Born--Infeld parity does not select
      $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ or identify
      $F_n$ with a Heisenberg or Weil carrier.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      Take $G_n=\mathfrak{S}_3$, $X=(12)$, $Y=(23)$, and let the involution
      $\sigma$ be conjugation by $(13)$.
      Then $\sigma(X)=Y$, $\langle X,Y\rangle=G_n$, and neither generator alone
      generates $G_n$.
      The standard action on the two-dimensional zero-sum subspace of
      $\mathbb{C}^3$ is faithful, unitary, and irreducible.
      Yet $[X,Y]=(132)\neq 1$ while $Z(\mathfrak{S}_3)=\{1\}$.
      Thus the commutator is non-trivial but non-central.
      The full contract audit is given in the companion
      paper~\cite{Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{equation}
      \begin{aligned}
        \substack{\text{A3 + representation}\\\text{dictionary}}
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{irreducibility},\\
        \text{irreducibility + }\dim F_n>1
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; [X,\sigma(X)]\neq 1,\\
        [X,\sigma(X)]\neq 1
        &\;\not\Longrightarrow\; [X,\sigma(X)]\in Z(G_n),\\
        \substack{\text{supplied Heisenberg group}\\
                  \text{+ central character}}
        &\;\Longrightarrow\; F_n\simeq\mathcal{H}_c
        \quad\text{(Stone--von Neumann).}
      \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    \begin{remark}[Boundary of the uncertainty claim]
      \label{rem:uncertainty}
      A Robertson-type inequality requires a supplied Hilbert carrier,
      self-adjoint observables, and their variances.
      Non-commutation alone neither selects those data nor guarantees a strictly
      positive lower bound in every state.
      The uncertainty principle is therefore conditional on the operator
      dictionary; it is not a corollary of A1--A3 alone.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Conditional canonical quantisation]
      A supplied finite symplectic vector space defines a Heisenberg central
      extension, after which Stone--von Neumann selects its irreducible carrier.
      Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg} shows that A1--A3 do not supply that symplectic
      input.
    \end{remark}



    A quantum superposition is read as an unresolved proto-state: a physically real
    configuration open across multiple admissible directions, whose coherent relations presuppose the
    supplied complex carrier and the additional reading of A3 discussed above.
    There is no ontological paradox — superposition is the normal condition of
    a system between two transitions.

    A measurement is a transition whose admissibility constraints are strong enough
    to force resolution of the proto-state onto a single admissible direction.
    Free evolution and measurement differ in the strength of their admissibility
    constraints, not in their fundamental mechanism.

    In this sense, quantum mechanics is the effective theory of physically real
    unresolved admissible structures and their resolution.

  \subsection{The wavefunction as effective representation of the proto-state}
    \label{subsec:wavefunction}

    A quantum wave is not a physical substance propagating in space.
    It is the effective representation of a physically real unresolved proto-state.
    It encodes the structure of admissible directions prior to projection locking, on the supplied carrier;
    its coherence rests on the additional reading of A3.

    More precisely: the proto-state $\Psi_n$ is real;
    the wavefunction $\psi$ is a coordinate description, on the supplied carrier, of the admissible
    structure carried by $\Psi_n$.
    In the conditional O-series realisation, coordinates may be taken in the
    supplied Heisenberg carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$; this is not an axiomatic
    identification of $F_n$.
    When we write $\psi$, we are not describing a substance propagating in space
    — we are encoding the relational phase structure of an unresolved
    configuration in the space of admissible directions.
    Quantum mechanics does not describe realized physical states:
    it describes states in the process of being resolved.

    This offers a reading of the standard interpretational difficulties; it rests on the supplied complex
    carrier, on the additional reading of A3 for coherence, and on the Born weight as an additional rule:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \emph{Superposition} is the normal condition of a proto-state — the
      simultaneous structural openness across all elements of $F_n$.
      \item \emph{Interference} is the consequence of coherent propagation
      through multiple admissible directions before resolution, under those inputs.
      \item \emph{Collapse} is not a physical process applied to a pre-existing state
      but the resolution of the proto-state at projection locking — the transition
      from unresolved admissible structure to a realized observable outcome.
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Complex amplitudes on the supplied carrier}
    \label{subsec:complex-amplitudes}

    Taking the proto-state to be a physically real, unresolved, coherent configuration, its coherence resting
    on the additional reading of A3 stated above, what is the minimal mathematical structure capable of
    representing it?

    A classical probability mixture over pre-existing alternatives does not represent relative
    phase relations: real positive weights describe ignorance about which alternative is realized,
    whereas the proto-state is taken to carry definite phase relations between its admissible
    directions.
    This rules out a classical probability mixture as the representation; it does not rule out real
    amplitudes.
    A real vector space with signed amplitudes supports coherent superposition and interference, and a
    complex doublet together with its SU(2) action can be written on four real coordinates.
    Whether complex amplitudes are minimal therefore depends on the category of representations
    admitted and on the rule for composing subsystems.
    Stueckelberg formulated quantum theory in a real Hilbert space, the imaginary unit being replaced by
    an operator commuting with all observables~\cite{Stueckelberg1960}.
    With the standard tensor-product composition and independent sources, real and complex quantum
    theory make different predictions in network scenarios~\cite{Renou2021}; with a different,
    physically motivated composition rule, a real formulation reproduces the predictions of all
    multipartite experiments~\cite{BarriosHita2026}.
    Quaternionic formulations also exist~\cite{Adler1995}.
    No general selection of complex amplitudes follows from coherence alone.

    In the present framework the complex structure enters with the supplied carriers: the
    Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger carrier $\mathcal{H}_c \cong \mathbb{C}^q$ of the O-series
    (Remark~\ref{rem:fn-vrho}) and the two-dimensional spinor carrier $\mathbb{C}^2$ supplied conditionally by
    O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
    The Weil module associated with $\mathcal{H}_c$ carries the further symplectic action used by the spectral
    construction (Remark~\ref{rem:fn-vrho}); the spinor carrier is not identified with it, O23 finding faithful
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-valued constituents of the restricted Weil module only for explicitly studied blocks and
    without a derived rule selecting one.
    Neither the Heisenberg--Schr\"odinger carrier nor the spinor carrier is derived from A1--A4, and invoking them
    to select complex over real amplitudes would be circular.
    On these supplied carriers, a proto-state is represented by a complex amplitude: the imaginary unit
    encodes the relative phase between admissible directions, and the SU(2) action of the spinor carrier
    acts on these amplitudes.
    A selection of complex amplitudes from the axioms would require an explicit category of
    representations, a minimality criterion and a composition rule; it is open.

  \subsection{The Born weight: requirements and status}
    \label{subsec:born-weight}

    Upon projection locking (A4), the unresolved proto-state is resolved onto
    a single admissible direction.
    The quantity associated with each direction at resolution — the
    \emph{intensity of realisation} — must satisfy four structural requirements:

    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item \emph{Positivity}: a weight on admissible directions cannot be negative.
\item \emph{Global phase invariance}: multiplying $\psi$ by a global phase
$e^{i\theta}$, available on the supplied complex carrier (Section~\ref{subsec:complex-amplitudes}), does not
change the physical proto-state, so the weight must be invariant under this transformation.
\item \emph{Normalisability}: the total weight over all admissible directions
must be finite and summable to one.
\item \emph{Coherence preservation before resolution}: the weight must not
erase phase relations prematurely — interference must remain possible up to
the moment of resolution; this requirement presupposes the additional reading of A3
(Section~\ref{sec:admissibility}).
    \end{enumerate}

    These requirements do not select the squared norm on their own.
    Every weight of the form $f(|\psi|)$ is invariant under global phase shifts, $|\psi|$ and $|\psi|^2$
    included, so (ii) does not separate them.
    Positivity and normalisability are met by normalising any positive function of $|\psi|$, so (i) and (iii)
    do not separate them either, and (iv) constrains the configuration before resolution rather than the
    weight assigned at resolution.
    Selecting $|\psi|^2$ requires a further input: Gleason's theorem derives the trace form of every measure
    additive over orthogonal decompositions, and hence the squared norm for pure states, on a Hilbert space of
    dimension at least three~\cite{Gleason1957}.
    Additivity over orthogonal decompositions is not among (i)--(iv) and is not derived from A1--A4, so the
    theorem does not apply here even on the carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$, whose dimension exceeds two; on the
    two-dimensional spinor carrier its dimension hypothesis fails as well.
    In the present framework the Born weight is therefore an additional rule on the supplied carrier: the
    squared norm is compatible with (i)--(iv), and no argument here derives it.

    \begin{remark}[Status of the Born-rule uniqueness argument]
  The requirements above are compatible with the squared norm as the resolution intensity of the
  proto-state but do not single it out.
  No companion paper in this
  programme currently supplies an independent uniqueness proof for this
  claim: the Born-rule derivation is not established by~\cite{Beau2026q1},
  whose proved content is a separate, unconditional rigidity theorem
  unrelated to the Born rule.
  The Born weight is an additional rule, and its derivation is open.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[The wavefunction lives in the admissible fibre, not in space]
  A common source of confusion is to picture the wavefunction as an object
  propagating in physical space.
  In the present framework, $\psi$ encodes the structure of $F_n$ — the
  admissible directions from $O_{n-1}$.
  In the O-series, its concrete realisation is conditionally modelled in the
  supplied Heisenberg carrier $\mathcal{H}_c$.
  The axioms do not identify $F_n$ with $\mathcal{H}_c$ or with the associated
  Weil module $\mathcal{W}$.
  The wavefunction does not propagate in space; it encodes the relational
  phase structure of an unresolved admissible configuration.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Projection locking and discreteness}
    \label{sec:locking}

    Under A3 and A4, resolution is discrete (Theorem~\ref{thm:discrete-locking}).
    This discreteness is not postulated: it follows from the interaction of two
    structures already present in the framework.

  \subsection{Two types of discreteness}

    The Born--Infeld saturation condition — the saturation candidate for the
    capacity bound of~\cite{Beau2026c} — operates on a continuous amplitude
    function.
    Its saturation locus $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BI}} \subset \mathbb{R}$ is
    generically real-valued — its elements carry no information about shell
    structure.

    The observable space decomposes discretely:
    \begin{equation}
      O \;=\; \bigsqcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}_q} O_n,
    \end{equation}
    indexed by BFS shells of the Heisenberg Cayley graph~\cite{Beau2026a26}.
    No component $O_{n'}$ exists for non-admissible depths.

    The proto-state is resolved at the first element of
    $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BI}} \cap \mathbb{N}_q$ — the first depth where
    Born--Infeld saturation coincides with an available shell.
    This is the Projection Locking theorem~\cite{Beau2026a26}.

    \begin{theorem}[Discreteness from projection locking, after O22]
      \label{thm:discrete-locking}
      Under A3 and A4, quantum transitions are discrete.
      The discreteness is a consequence of the interaction of continuous
      Born--Infeld saturation with the discrete admissibility filter of $\Pi_n$,
      not a postulate.
    \end{theorem}

  \subsection{Three stable directions}

    For a supplied irreducible two-dimensional unitary carrier
    $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ (the spinor carrier of O23, in its notation; not the Weil module $\mathcal{W}$ of
    Remark~\ref{rem:fn-vrho}), the traceless neutral sector is
    $\mathfrak{su}(2) \cong \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H}$, of real dimension
    exactly three (\cite{Beau2026a27}, Theorem~3.1).
    The chain connecting this theorem to the Born--Infeld structure and to
    the observable threshold is status-typed as follows:
    \begin{equation}
      \begin{aligned}
        \text{BI parity (covariant, proved)}
        &\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{open}\;}\; \text{fibre involution } c \leftrightarrow q-c \\
        &\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{open}\;}\; V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2
        \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{proved}\;}\; \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{H} = 3 \\
        &\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{open}\;}\; \Sigma_c(n_3) = 3.
      \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    Only the adjoint-dimension arrow is a theorem; the fibre identification is
    the open classification problem of~\cite{Beau2026a22}, and the threshold
    $\Sigma_c(n_3) = 3$ is a supplied selection rule~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
    The reading of exactly three stable directions — and with it, three
    particle generations — as algebraic in origin is conditional on the two
    open bridges.

  \section{Entanglement and the Open Bell-Factorisation Bridge}
  \label{sec:entanglement}

  \subsection{Entanglement as a shared infra-projectable cell}

    Two observable systems $A$ and $B$ are entangled when their joint description occupies a single
    non-decomposable cell of the projection $\Pi_n$: a fibre that $\Pi_n$ cannot factorise into separate
    projected descriptions of $A$ and $B$.
    The correlation between them is exactly the part of the joint fibre that $\Pi_n$ cannot split.

    This is a synchronic, structural property of the non-injective projection, not a residue of a past
    interaction.
    The binding is the non-factorisability of the fibre forced by A1 and A3 under the representation dictionary
    of Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation};
    it holds wherever a shared non-decomposable cell is present, independently of how that cell arose.
    For the conjugate Weil pair $\{c,q-c\}$ that organises the spectral cascade, with $V_c$ and $V_{q-c}$ the
    corresponding Weil blocks of $\mathcal{W}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:fn-vrho}), the binding is explicit and
    permanent: the two characters are related by complex conjugation,
    $V_{q-c}\simeq\overline{V_c}$ (the Born--Infeld parity candidate), which holds at every depth $n$.
    The pair is a single closed object throughout the cascade, not two subsystems that were joined at an
    earlier transition and have since separated.

    A conditional quantitative account is available. For the conjugate pair, the minimal admissible
    pair carrier is fixed by an explicit atomic-carrier hypothesis — (H-carrier)
    in~\cite{Beau2026ent2}, derived nowhere in the corpus; its rank-three input is the supplied
    selection rule of~\cite{Beau2026a27} — under which the pair sector is the two-dimensional
    Born--Infeld parity pair, identified with the spin-$\tfrac12$ representation. Under a further,
    independent explicit hypothesis that the joint state is invariant under the diagonal action of
    the relevant finite group — not derived from admissibility, stated and used conditionally
    in~\cite{Beau2026q3} — its reduced state is the singlet, with entanglement entropy
    $\log 2$~\cite{Beau2026ent2}. Neither hypothesis, nor the resulting entropy value, is
    established unconditionally by Axioms A1--A4 alone.
    A finer pipeline-level observable, the full residual fibre-level rank $r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$, gives
    $S_{\mathrm{ent}}(n)=\log r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$, proved for matched single-character blocks and conditional
    otherwise on a residual indiscernibility hypothesis~\cite{Beau2026entnote,Beau2026ent1}.
    The two observables must not be conflated; they coincide only when $r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)=2$.

    The invariant carried by the shared cell is infra-projectable: it has no position in the projected
    temporal ordering, which is itself the cumulative projected capacity and, as an ordering, is
    conditional on \textup{[H-acyc]}.
    It is a single unsplit object below the resolution of $\Pi_n$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness}),
    not a pair of separately ordered events.
    In this sense entanglement is the not-yet-time-ordered sector of the cascade, rather than a record of a past
    joining.
    Correlations of this kind carry no signal and admit no influence between separated projected descriptions;
    locality at the projected level is preserved.

  \subsection{What a Bell-factorisation result would require}

    Bell's theorem~\cite{Bell1964} concerns the existence of a setting-independent variable
    $\lambda$ for which
    \[
      P(a,b|A,B,\lambda)=P(a|A,\lambda)P(b|B,\lambda).
    \]
    The variable is not required to be reconstructible from observable data.
    In particular, an underlying configuration $\omega$ is itself an admissible candidate
    for~$\lambda$.

    Non-injectivity only states that observable data do not determine~$\omega$ uniquely.
    It does not prevent conditioning on~$\omega$ in a candidate Bell-local completion.
    For example, let settings at $A$ and $B$ take values $x,y\in\{0,1\}$ respectively, and let
    \[
      \Omega=\{-1,+1\}^{4}\times\{0,1\},
      \qquad
      \Pi_{xy}(A_0,A_1,B_0,B_1,r)=(A_x,B_y),
    \]
    with the uniform measure.
    Every map~$\Pi_{xy}$ has fibres of size eight, yet $\lambda=\omega$ gives deterministic
    local response functions, measurement independence, and CHSH value zero.
    This countermodel disproves the implication from non-injectivity to failure of Bell
    factorisation claimed in~\cite{Beau2026b}; the general no-go theorem and the identification
    of the correct obstruction are proved in~\cite{Beau2026BellNoGo}.

    The missing input is a compatibility obstruction for the complete family of
    measurement contexts: equivalently, the absence of a setting-independent global
    coupling over local response assignments.
    A shared infra-projectable cell may motivate such an obstruction, but Axioms A1--A4
    do not currently derive it.
    Bell non-factorisability is therefore open in the present framework.

  \subsection*{Partial spectral stratification: an organising pattern from the companion programme}
    \label{ssec:spectral-stratification}

    The four axioms A1--A4 are the axiomatic primitive of the programme.
    They do not by themselves determine the interaction content of the emergent theory;
    that content emerges from the spectral structure of the admissible operator,
    developed in the Q-series companion papers.
    On a supplied Riemannian metric, for a supplied compact gauge group, with the renormalized couplings
    supplied as matching data, under the infrared conditions of Gravity (a non-zero matched Einstein
    coefficient and $R L_4^2 \ll 1$) and in the proper-time cutoff scheme,
    the companion papers~\cite{Beau2026h,Beau2026q12,Beau2026q13} support
    the following partial pattern, which organises the physical interpretation
    of the Seeley--DeWitt expansion of $S_\Pi[g,A]$:

    \begin{principle}[Spectral stratification of effective interactions]
      \label{prin:stratification}
      The physical role of an emergent interaction is determined not only by the
      symmetry it carries, but by the spectral order at which the admissible operator
      responds to variation.
      The gravitational sector appears at the first non-trivial horizontal response;
      the gauge sector appears at the vertical curvature response;
      and dimension-six mixed gauge--gravity invariants occur at $a_6$, where only their inventory is
      available.
    \end{principle}

    The evidence base for Principle~\ref{prin:stratification} is as follows:

    \[
      \begin{array}{c|c|l}
        \text{Spectral order} & \text{Sector} & \text{Geometric origin} \\
        \hline
        a_2 & \text{gravity}
        & \text{base-metric variation,} \\
        & & \text{cutoff scheme}~\text{\cite{Beau2026h}} \\
        a_4 & \text{gauge}
        & \text{vertical variation of the} \\
        & & \text{fibre connection}~\text{\cite{Beau2026q12}} \\
        a_6 & \text{mixed invariants}
        & \text{inventory only}~\text{\cite{Beau2026q13}} \\
        a_{\mathrm{spin}} & \text{fermionic structure}
        & \text{Dirac-type admissible operator (open)} \\
      \end{array}
    \]

    \begin{remark}
      \label{rem:stratification-status}
      Principle~\ref{prin:stratification} is \emph{not} derived from A1--A4 alone.
      Its correct epistemic status is: supported by the companion programme under the conditions stated
      above, consistent with A1--A4, and subject to the conditionalities of each companion paper.
      The separation of divergence degrees, quadratic at $a_2$ and logarithmic at $a_4$, holds in the
      proper-time cutoff scheme and is not scheme-independent~\cite{Beau2026h,Beau2026q12}; no hierarchy of
      the physical couplings is predicted, $G_N$ and $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ being independent matching
      data~\cite{Beau2026q13}, and the derivation is Euclidean.
      In particular, the gauge group $G_\Pi$ is a supplied input of the $a_4$ row: no identification
      $G_\Pi = \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ is established.
      O31 version~2.0 is a withdrawal notice that asserts no derivation of a gauge factor or of that
      group~\cite{Beau2026a35}, and the O32 measurements identify no group~\cite{Beau2026a36}.
      The open $a_{\mathrm{spin}}$ row would be tested by whether admissibility forces a Clifford module
      and a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading on the admissible operator.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection*{Open directions}

    \paragraph{The Born rule, and quantum structure beyond the finite fibre.}
      The Born rule $P(a=+1|\hat{a}) = |\langle +\hat{a}|\psi\rangle|^2$ is not
      derived by any companion paper of this programme; it enters as an additional rule
      (Section~\ref{subsec:born-weight}), not as a proved uniqueness theorem.
      The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026q2} establishes an exact finite-group fact
      — the spin-$\tfrac12$ and spin-$\tfrac32$ representations of the binary
      icosahedral group $2I$ induce the same Cayley-graph Laplacian eigenvalue —
      but does not derive phase coherence, a singlet correlator, a Tsirelson bound,
      the Born rule, or a sector-selection argument from it.
      The companion
      paper~\cite{Beau2026q3} proves a conditional theorem — given an explicit,
      undemonstrated diagonal-invariance hypothesis, the joint state of two copies
      of an admissible sector is the SU(2) singlet, with an explicit Casimir
      correlator — but does not derive that hypothesis, phase coherence, or the
      Born rule.
      Deriving the Born rule, deriving phase coherence and extending a
      correlator to arbitrary observables and multipartite systems without
      importing an undemonstrated invariance hypothesis, and identifying a
      physically preferred sector, all remain open.

    \paragraph{O31/O32 --- Colour sector and recursive non-injectivity.}
      The SU(2) sector is read on conjugate pairs $\{c, q-c\}$, under the carrier supplied
      conditionally by O23~\cite{Beau2026a27}.
      The supplied finite-field carrier admits triplets
      $\{c_1, c_2, c_3\}$ satisfying $c_1 + c_2 + c_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{q}$
      and pairwise non-conjugacy, for every prime $q \geq 7$~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
      No result identifies such triplets with a colour sector: O31 version~2.0 asserts no
      triplet co-admissibility and no derivation of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ or of
      $G_{\mathrm{SM}} = \mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$~\cite{Beau2026a35},
      and O29 identifies no spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ module in its measured spaces~\cite{Beau2026a33}.
      The identification of quarks as stable projected configurations in
      $\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi$ carrying colour and spin quantum numbers, and the derivation of
      colour confinement as a structural consequence of the projection
      $\Pi_{\mathrm{colour}} : \{c_i\} \to \{\text{colour-neutral objects}\}$
      being non-injective (ENI~\cite{Beau2026l}, Corollary~6), are the primary
      open directions of the colour sector.

      More broadly, ENI Corollary~6 proves non-injectivity at every stratum of a tower of
      projections under its own premises on each projection: it is surjective, the observables of
      its level are defined by it, and that level is not isomorphic to the level below; the
      projection entropies then add across strata.
      These premises are not consequences of A1--A4, and their verification for the strata
      proposed below is open, as it is for the local map of A2 (Remark~\ref{rem:a2-status}).
      The programme proposes two strata: the
      pair level (leptons, $\Pi_1$) and the triplet level (colour sector,
      $\Pi_2$), whose colour identification is open.
      The structural derivation of the full particle hierarchy from this
      recursive tower is the central open direction of the programme.

    \paragraph{Spectral quasi-fixed points and stable structures.}
      The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(O_n)$ varies generically with $n$
      (Remark~\ref{rem:projective-position}).
      However, configurations $O^*$ for which $\mathcal{A}(O^*)$ is approximately
      self-similar --- in the sense that projection from $O^*$ tends to return to a
      spectrally close neighbourhood --- define \emph{spectral quasi-fixed points} of
      the admissible flow.
      These are not static states, but projective orbits that traverse the same spectral region
      repeatedly.
      What excludes staticity is the novelty condition of Section~\ref{subsec:ni-novelty}, under
      which a successor is indexed as a distinct state only when $\Delta_n \neq 0$.
      Immediate information loss would not exclude it: a non-injective $\Pi_n$ can identify
      several configurations and still return the same observable state, which is precisely the
      cycle left open by \textup{[H-acyc]}.
      Stable physical structures --- particles, gauge symmetries, and coupling constants ---
      are candidates for identification with such quasi-fixed points: their observed
      stability would then be a projective property, arising from the near-invariance of
      their admissible neighbourhood under repeated projection, not from any ontological
      fixity.
      The universality of $a_q(s) \cdot q^2 \approx 2$ across all tested primes
      (O-series numerics) is consistent with the existence of such an attractor in
      the admissible spectral flow, independent of~$q$.
      The companion paper Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} does not turn this observation into a
      coefficient.
      The $\mathrm{su}(2)$ Casimir acts on the spin-$1$ module $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$, $V_\rho \cong \mathbb{C}^2$
      being the spinor carrier of O23 (not the Weil module $\mathcal{W}$ of Remark~\ref{rem:fn-vrho}), as
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:casimir-2}
        C_{\mathrm{su}(2)}\big|_{j=1} \;=\; j(j+1) \;=\; 2,
      \end{equation}
      but invariance fixes an invariant quadratic form only up to a positive factor, so the
      value $2$ determines no coefficient of the effective spatial co-metric.
      The route that would connect them reads:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:casimir-chain}
        \begin{aligned}
        &\mathrm{A2}
        \;\Rightarrow\; \text{non-trivial fibre}
        \;\Rightarrow\; [\mathrm{U}]
        \;\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{su}(2)\text{-isotropy}
        \\
        &\qquad\;\Rightarrow\; C_{\mathrm{su}(2)}|_{j=1} = 2
        \;\Rightarrow\; A_H(q) \to 2,
        \end{aligned}
      \end{equation}
      where only the first implication is established (Corollary~\ref{cor:projective-incompleteness}).
      The uniformity hypothesis~$[\mathrm{U}]$ is not established: conjugation parity and
      concentrated pair exponents do not imply it~\cite{Beau2026q10}.
      Under $[\mathrm{U}]$, Q10 proves that two depth-weighted averages of the fingerprint
      observable have a ratio within $2\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$ of one; this becomes a
      statement about the horizontal and central coefficients only through four inputs that
      Q10 names and does not supply (a sector assignment of blocks to spin weights, a linear
      response law, a positive rank-three target form and an independent absolute scale), and
      no coefficient value, in particular no limit $A_H(q) \to 2$, is derived.
      The value $2$ therefore remains a numerical observation, not a consequence of the
      representation theory of the admissible fibre, and no representation-theoretic
      interpretation of the quasi-fixed point is established.
      Formalising the existence and classification of quasi-fixed points of
      $\mathcal{A}$ is an open direction of the programme.

      Theorem~\ref{thm:heisenberg} establishes that the axioms do not select the
      finite Heisenberg carrier.
      Conditional on supplying that carrier, its relationship to continuous
      spacetime geometry in the large-$q$ limit — including the construction of
      a scaling map from $\mathcal{H}_c$ to $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and the emergence
      of a four-dimensional effective geometry from BFS shells —
      is addressed in the companion spectral geometry paper~\cite{Beau2026q5a}
      (Section~\ref{subsec:continuum-limit}).
      That paper identifies the canonical filtration exactly (a growing
      toric Fourier window), proves that the published admissibility form converges to
      the zero form on it, and proves a normalisation no-go: under its stated conditions on the
      saturation depth and the weights, no common scalar normalisation produces a non-trivial
      toric differential operator.
      Q5 therefore remains open.
      The identification of the effective metric from the principal symbol of a limit
      operator $L_\Pi$ and the Lorentzian signature selection (Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b})
      are conditional on the unestablished spatial limit hypothesis [H-L] and on the open lifting
      hypothesis [H-lift] of Q5b; the signature result also rests on the hyperbolicity hypothesis
      of~\cite{Beau2026c} and gives $(-,+,+)$ on the rank-three non-degenerate block only, a Lorentzian
      $(-,+,+,+)$ signature requiring a full-rank extension that is not constructed.
      The temporal coefficient is not fixed: Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11} proves that invariance under a
      spatial $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ action leaves the ratio $A_\tau/A_H$ unconstrained, so $A_\tau = 2$
      is not derived on that route; one datum coupling the temporal and spatial normalisations
      is missing.

    \paragraph{Q14 --- Fermionic structure and chirality.}
      The spectral stratification of Principle~\ref{prin:stratification} identifies
      the next open level of the programme: the fermionic sector.
      On a supplied metric and a supplied compact gauge group, Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12} derives the source-free
      Yang--Mills equations from a Laplace-type admissible operator $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$, and
      Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13} joins them with the conditional Einstein sector of Gravity~\cite{Beau2026h}
      into a conditional, Euclidean Einstein--Yang--Mills system whose couplings are matching data.
      Fermionic structure requires a Dirac-type operator $\mathcal{D}_{g,A}$
      satisfying $\mathcal{D}^2 = \mathcal{A}_{g,A} + \tfrac{1}{4}R$
      (Lichnerowicz formula), whose $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading encodes chirality.
      The central open question is whether the admissibility structure forces a
      Clifford module structure on the admissible fibre and thereby selects a
      $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading: if so, the chiral asymmetry of the Standard Model
      becomes a structural consequence of admissibility; if not, this constitutes
      a structural boundary of the programme.

  \section{Relation to Established Frameworks}
  \label{sec:frameworks}

  The four axioms A1--A4 constitute a proposed primitive layer from which geometry, dynamics,
  and quantum structure are intended to emerge; in the present paper they emerge only in part, on supplied
  carriers, and the geometric continuum is conditional on the open hypothesis [H-L].
  It is natural to ask how this primitive relates to the established formalisms of
  theoretical physics — not in the sense of reduction, but in the sense of structural
  translation: what each framework corresponds to, where it appears in the emergent
  hierarchy, and what it presupposes that the present framework derives, takes conditionally, or supplies.

  \paragraph{Hamilton--Jacobi dynamics.}
    Once the effective metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ has been reconstructed from the principal
    symbol of the admissibility operator (Q5b, Q6b~\cite{Beau2026q5b,Beau2026q6b};
    conditional on the spatial limit hypothesis [H-L] and the lifting hypothesis [H-lift]),
    the characteristic propagation equation of the effective geometry coincides with the
    Hamilton--Jacobi equation for massless propagation.
    This identification belongs to the already projected geometric regime, downstream of
    the admissibility reconstruction.
    Hamilton--Jacobi theory should therefore be understood as an effective reformulation
    of the projected dynamics — valid once the effective configuration space has been
    constructed by the primitive admissibility relation — not as a primitive layer of the
    framework.
    The detailed identification, including the eikonal equation as principal-symbol
    characteristic, is given in Q6b~\cite{Beau2026q6b}.

  \paragraph{Symplectic geometry and analytical mechanics.}
    The framework of symplectic geometry takes a phase space — a cotangent bundle
    $T^*M$ equipped with a canonical symplectic form — as its fundamental arena.
    In the present framework, this phase space is not a primitive: the effective
    configuration space $M$ would emerge from the Carnot--Carath\'eodory geometry of the
    BFS shell stratification (Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}), conditionally on the open spatial limit hypothesis [H-L],
    and the cotangent structure would be induced by the principal symbol of the admissibility operator,
    under the further open lifting hypothesis [H-lift].
    Symplectic geometry is therefore the appropriate language for the effective dynamics
    on $T^*M$, valid after the configuration space has been constructed by the
    admissibility constraints; it does not describe the admissibility structure that
    produces it.

  \paragraph{WKB approximation and geometric optics.}
    In the WKB approximation, the quantum wave function takes the form
    $\psi \sim A\,e^{iS/\hbar}$, where the phase $S$ is the Hamilton--Jacobi action.
    In the present framework, the phase of an admissible mode is defined on the supplied carrier of the
    conditional spectral model; neither the phase nor its coherence is derived from the axioms.
    The WKB identification therefore holds at the level of the effective description,
    once the continuum limit has been taken --- an operation currently conditional on
    the open hypothesis [H-L] of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}, Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} giving no spatial
    limit operator: the collective
    phase of the admissible modes plays the role of $S$, and geometric optics is the
    ray approximation of the admissible propagation structure in this limit.
    The WKB phase is thus an effective quantity defined on the supplied carrier, rather than a fundamental
    dynamical object of the framework.

  \paragraph{Functional renormalisation group.}
    The Wetterich effective average action satisfies an exact renormalisation group
    equation whose infrared fixed point recovers the physical effective action.
    In the present framework, the conjectured Mosco limit of the admissibility
    Dirichlet forms (hypothesis [H-L]; Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} shows the
    published normalisation yields the zero form) would play a structurally analogous
    role: the sequence $\{\mathcal{E}_q\}$ would flow, as $q\to\infty$, to a scalar
    operator $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ whose principal symbol defines the effective metric under the further
    open lifting hypothesis [H-lift] of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b}.
    The spectral entropy functional $S_\Pi[g,A]$ of the companion programme would be the
    effective action resulting from this limit; the companion papers evaluate it on a supplied metric and
    supplied gauge data, not on a constructed limit operator~\cite{Beau2026h,Beau2026q12,Beau2026q13}.
    The intended structural difference is that the Wetterich flow renormalises a pre-existing field
    theory, while the present limit would produce the field content itself from the admissibility
    constraints; that production is not established.

  \paragraph{Page--Wootters construction and relational time.}
    The Page--Wootters construction derives apparent temporal evolution from conditional
    states of a globally stationary wave function, treating time as a relational label on
    a clock subsystem.
    The projective temporal ordering of the present framework~\cite{Beau2026pto} shares
    this relational spirit — time would be, not a parameter of the substrate, but a monotone
    ordering induced by the admissibility cascade, an ordering that is itself conditional on
    \textup{[H-acyc]} — but differs in one structural respect.
    In the Page--Wootters model, the global state is fully recoverable (the projection is
    effectively injective), whereas here the projection $\Pi$ is structurally non-injective
    by Axiom~A2 (the ENI premises~\cite{Beau2026l} are not established for it,
    Remark~\ref{rem:a2-status}).
    Along the non-injective transitions A2 asserts to be generic, no step of the present framework
    is reversible: what the comparison rests on is the accumulation of projected spectral capacity,
    not the choice of a clock observable.
    That these steps compose into any ordering at all, global arrow included, is conditional on
    \textup{[H-acyc]}.

  \paragraph{Holographic renormalisation and radial Hamilton--Jacobi.}
    In holographic renormalisation, the on-shell gravitational action satisfies a radial
    Hamilton--Jacobi equation whose flow encodes the renormalisation group of the boundary
    field theory.
    Q6b~\cite{Beau2026q6b} assembles the effective geometry conditionally: under [H-L] and the open [H-lift],
    for a supplied rank-four extension that is spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat and asymptotically
    homogeneous, flux conservation gives the leading Newtonian exterior profile, after the Born--Infeld
    paper~\cite{Beau2026c}; the Schwarzschild form follows only with external dynamical input, no uniqueness is
    claimed, and the horizon reading is interpretive.
    Read in this way, the construction has a structural parallel with this picture: both frameworks would
    obtain a bulk geometry from a propagation constraint.
    The structural difference is that holographic renormalisation presupposes a fixed
    anti-de Sitter background and a specific bulk-boundary duality, while the present
    framework would derive the effective geometry from the admissibility operator without a background or a
    boundary field theory, conditionally on [H-L] and on supplied carriers.
